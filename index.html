About me

I am a mathematician with a background in dynamical systems, probability theory and topology. My work lies at the intersection of mathematical theory, statistical modeling, and computational methods.

Throughout my academic career, I have explored problems in ergodic theory, topological dynamics, and mathematical physics. I enjoy both the abstract development of mathematical ideas and their translation into concrete models and simulations.

At the German Aerospace Center (DLR) in Cologne, I work on Python-based simulations of materials science problems and the mathematical analysis of the underlying models. From 2024 to 2026, I took a research leave to pursue an independently acquired project funded by the German Research Foundation (DFG) through the Walter Benjamin Programme at the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile in Santiago. Before joining the DLR, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Mathematics in Bonn.

My PhD research at Friedrich Schiller University Jena focused on extending classical entropy theory — a framework for quantifying dynamical complexity — to the study of long-range order in materials and mathematical quasicrystals.

I have been passionate about software development since my youth, beginning with the independent development of simulation software for Fraunhofer IVV during my school years.

In my free time, I enjoy hiking and making music. I play the saxophone and guitar.
